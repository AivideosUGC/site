From Raw Product to High-Converting Ad: The Six-Step Workflow

The workflow below is the operational spine of an AI ecommerce video pipeline. It is designed so that every step is reviewable before the next one locks in—brand guidelines and product accuracy are enforced at the storyboard stage, not discovered after render.

Step 1: Add Product

Start with the asset you already have. Paste a live product URL (Shopify, Amazon, or another marketplace), or upload a high-resolution product image directly into the dashboard. This is the input that anchors every downstream scene, script, and claim.

Step 2: Choose a Scene and Presenter

Select from a matrix of pre-configured, conversion-tested industry scenes—Beauty, Fashion, Tech, and others—and pair them with a photorealistic AI presenter that matches your brand persona. The scene sets the visual grammar; the presenter sets the voice and on-camera authority.

Step 3: Describe Product, Audience, and Creative Idea

Feed the system three inputs: the core value proposition, the target consumer demographics, and the creative direction or promotional angle. This is where the ad stops being generic. A script written for “busy parents who need a 10-second cleanup” will outperform a generic “premium quality” prompt every time.

Step 4: Save and Refine the Storyboard

Review each generated shot, visual prompt, and spoken script line side-by-side. Lock in brand guidelines—tone, claims, product representation, and any required disclaimers—before triggering the final render. This is the control point that separates a scalable pipeline from an unpredictable generator.

Step 5: Generate and Assemble Shots

Render selected shots programmatically, compare different visual variations, and arrange the chosen high-performing clips into a cohesive timeline. Variant velocity matters: Meta’s post-Andromeda algorithm rewards creative volume and diversification, and AI collapses the production loop from weeks to hours (Admiral Media, 13-account test).

Step 6: Review and Download

Preview the complete video in real time, verifying voiceover pacing, audio clarity, and exact product visual details. Export the final high-definition file for paid advertising, social feeds, or the product landing page.

The 2026 Compliance Layer: AI Disclosure and AI Twin Authorization

Scale without disclosure is a regulatory liability, not a growth strategy. Two frameworks matter for any brand deploying AI avatars or AI twins in 2026.

IAB AI Transparency and Disclosure Framework (January 2026)

The IAB released the industry’s first AI transparency framework on January 15, 2026 (IAB press release). It uses a risk-based model: blanket labeling is not required, but disclosure is called for when AI materially affects authenticity, identity, or representation in ways that may mislead consumers. The framework specifically names synthetic humans, digital twins, AI-generated voices, and AI chatbots as categories that may require consumer-facing labels or machine-readable metadata (IAB). Authorized digital twins used in conventional brand endorsements can fall outside the automatic-disclosure category, but the disclosure decision should be documented, not assumed.

EU AI Act Article 50 (Effective August 2, 2026)

The EU has introduced transparency obligations for AI-generated content under Article 50 of the AI Act, applying from 2 August 2026 (artificialintelligenceact.eu). Deployers using AI to create deepfakes must disclose that content has been artificially generated or manipulated, and providers of generative AI systems must mark outputs in a machine-readable format (EU AI Act Article 50 guide). Brands targeting EU consumers should treat this as a hard compliance date for synthetic media labeling and disclosure workflows.

AI Twin authorization is non-negotiable

An AI twin is a clone of a real person. The legal basis for using it is authorization from that person—not ownership of a photo, not a past endorsement contract, and not a verbal agreement. For founder-led brands, the founder can authorize their own twin. For ambassador twins, the license should specify scope, duration, content categories, and revocation rights. The compliance burden is manageable, but it starts before the first render, not after.
